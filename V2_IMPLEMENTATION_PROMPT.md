# V2 implementation prompt · Pli d’Amine

Extend the existing French-language React/Vite eFootball standings app to calculate league standings from individual match-week scores and show the knockout stage. Use the frontend-only architecture, existing SheetJS (`xlsx`) dependency, and static GitHub Pages deployment.

## Confirmed requirements

1. **Start from V1.** Read `src/main.jsx`, `src/standings.js`, and `README.md`. V1 fetched the workbook now stored at `public/assets/results/Pli d Amine.xlsx` and reads precomputed standings from the first worksheet. It distinguishes the tournament team (`Nom d'équipe`) from the player name and base club (`Équipe de base`). Reuse the table, French labels, responsive behavior, and loading/retry pattern where practical. Keep the V1 workbook intact and never mix its precomputed totals with match-derived totals.

2. **Discover workbooks through a static manifest.** Place a JSON manifest under `public/assets/results/`, for example `match-weeks.json`. List each week in display order with a unique week ID, a French label, and its workbook filename. Document how to add a week by copying the file and adding its manifest entry. Validate manifest entries, reject repeated IDs or filenames, and keep paths local to the assets folder. Resolve the manifest and encoded filenames using Vite’s `import.meta.env.BASE_URL` so loading works in development and under a deployed project subpath. Fetch only listed files, check HTTP responses, read each workbook as an `ArrayBuffer`, and cancel stale requests on retry or unmount.

3. **Define the workbook contract from the supplied sample.** The owner will provide a real `GW1.xlsx` before V2 implementation. Inspect it and document the worksheet, header row, exact French column names or other cell layout, and data types before writing the parser. The parser must resolve two tournament team identities and two goal totals per played match. Take the week from the manifest unless the confirmed workbook contract provides it, and verify agreement if both sources identify the week. Use SheetJS raw cell values and preserve source filenames and Excel row numbers for diagnostics. Normalize incidental whitespace and Unicode consistently while keeping display labels. Accept zero as a score and validate goals as non-negative safe integers. Treat blank scores according to the confirmed fixture convention.

4. **Aggregate validated league matches.** Use one record per team and count each accepted match once for both teams. Increment matches played for both teams and add each side’s score to its goals for and the opponent’s goals against. Credit a win and three points to the winner and a loss to the loser. For a draw, credit both teams one draw and one point each. Calculate goal difference as goals for minus goals against. Sort by points, then goal difference, then goals for, all descending. Keep knockout results out of league totals.

5. **Display results and qualification.** Group match results by week and show both teams and the score, with a distinct pending state for confirmed unplayed fixtures. Show ranks 1–7 as provisionally qualified while league data is incomplete. Once the final standings are known, send rank 1 directly to the semi-finals and ranks 2–7 to three quarter-finals. The three confirmed quarter-final winners join rank 1 in the semi-finals. Leave unresolved slots pending and require a confirmed winner before advancing any team.

6. **Handle bad or incomplete data visibly.** Separate unavailable or unreadable files and invalid worksheet structure from row-level errors. Report missing teams, invalid or partial scores, self-matches, duplicates, and conflicting results with French messages that identify the week, file, and row when available. Use the confirmed fixture identity to detect duplicates across files while preserving legitimate rematches. Count identical copies once and exclude conflicting records until resolved. Keep valid results visible if some data fails, label the table « Classement provisoire », and show a distinct empty state if no valid matches are available. Example messages include « Impossible de charger la journée 2 », « GW1.xlsx, ligne 8 — Score manquant » and « Résultats contradictoires pour ce match ».

## Details to confirm before coding the affected behavior

- The real GW1 workbook layout, including worksheet, headers or cell positions, team identifiers or aliases, score fields or combined score notation, and how scheduled, postponed, or cancelled fixtures are represented.
- The source of the team roster and player/base-club metadata, whether teams with zero matches should appear, how rematches are distinguished, and how to tell a partially published week from a completed week. Confirm the full schedule or another explicit completion signal.
- The tie-break policy when all three ranking criteria are equal, especially at ranks 1 and 7. Flag unresolved ties that affect qualification or seeding until the owner confirms a rule.
- Quarter-final pairings for ranks 2–7, semi-final pairings, the source of knockout scores or winners, and the rule for deciding a winner when a knockout score is tied.

## Implementation and verification

Keep workbook loading, parsing, league calculations, and bracket progression separate from React rendering. Document the confirmed workbook contract, manifest update process, error and duplicate policy, and any agreed assumptions in the README. Verify the supplied sample, multiple weeks, wins and draws including 0–0, all three sorting criteria, duplicates and conflicts, partial weeks, and advancement of three quarter-final winners plus rank 1. Run the production build and check that the manifest and workbooks load from a static project subpath.
